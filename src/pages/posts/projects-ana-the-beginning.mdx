**Hi everyone!**


Welcome to what I hope will be the first of many entries documenting the journey of **Project ANA – Advanced Neural Adhesive**.


---


### **What is ANA?**


ANA stands for _Advanced Neural Adhesive_, a project focused on developing a **wearable, skin-like EEG sensor** that adheres to the forehead like a sticker. The vision is to create a seamless, comfortable, and truly unobtrusive neural interface that captures real-time brain activity—tracking cognitive states such as **focus, stress, mood, and fatigue**.


Unlike traditional EEG systems that are bulky, uncomfortable, and impractical for daily use, ANA aims to be **lightweight**, **biocompatible**, and **aesthetically invisible**, making neural tracking as effortless as wearing a patch. The device will wirelessly stream data via BLE to an app for processing and visual feedback, empowering users to “know themselves and change their world.”


We’re inspired by advances in flexible electronics, conductive polymers, and bio-integrated materials—and we’re trying to fuse all these innovations into a single cohesive product that’s actually usable outside the lab.

### **The Reason for This Blog**


There are two main reasons I’m doing this:


**First**, it’s just fun. Having a log of everything we’re doing—both the technical and the conceptual—helps us appreciate how our ideas evolve. It’s fascinating to see how your thinking changes the deeper you get into a field. And let’s be honest: we’ve been thrown straight into the deep end. But honestly? That’s the best way to learn. Our team genuinely believes that.


**Second**, this log will serve a practical purpose for the **FoNS-MAD competition**. We’re required to provide a detailed report on what we’ve done and how we’ve done it. This running documentation will help us reflect on our decisions, retrace our steps, and clearly show the progression of our work.


---


We’ll try to cover as much as we can—without slowing down the actual development. This is also a bit of a passion project. We want other students to see that you _don’t_ have to be an expert to start something meaningful. We’re just undergrads—learning on the fly, working smart, and building off the knowledge of those who came before us.


It may seem like a lot of effort, but hey—anything worth doing is worth doing well.


Let’s see how long we can keep this up 😄
