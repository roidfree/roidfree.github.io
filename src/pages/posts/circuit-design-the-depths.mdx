# Genuinely, uhh what?


Out of everything that needed to come together for this project, it was actually the chance to venture into electrical engineering and circuit design that excited—and terrified—me the most. It was this leap, confronting something entirely new, that truly motivated me to dive deeper.


Initially, this motivation was sparked by a sort of existential anxiety: the infamous "AI is taking over" crisis. Early this year, I convinced myself that my software development skills were rapidly losing value. AI seemed to be absorbing everything software-related at an alarming rate, prompting me to urgently expand my technical skillset into something AI couldn’t yet replicate—hardware design and electrical engineering. Classic AI anxiety moment, right?


Though I'd experimented with basic electronics before—like Arduino kits when I was 11 or 12, building simple robots or following YouTube tutorials for RFID card copiers—that experience felt safe and straightforward. This time was fundamentally different; I was going into unknown territory, genuinely unsure of what to expect.


To tackle this daunting new challenge, I broke it down into smaller, manageable steps:

1. **Choosing the Right Components:**
    - Initially overwhelmed, I started by looking at simple Bluetooth modules like the AT-09 but soon realized I needed more capable solutions like Nordic Semiconductor’s nRF52832 or Dialog’s DA14531 to achieve reliable EEG data transmission.
2. **Understanding IC Packages:**
    - Suddenly terms like "LQFP," "QFN," and "BGA" started appearing everywhere, making me realize that mastering hardware meant getting comfortable with physical components—their dimensions, capabilities, and limitations.
3. **Prototyping and Simulation:**
    - I learned about development boards and simulation tools like KiCad and LTspice, understanding their essential role in minimizing costly mistakes. This became a structured roadmap: dev boards → modular prototypes → simulations → custom PCB → miniaturized flexible circuit.

This structured approach not only calmed my initial anxieties but also significantly boosted my confidence as I started seeing tangible progress.


## It looked pretty cool, Tony Stark type cool


<Image src="/images/posts/971318e4432aecffc5c32fa1d6f907676ef2919b036897ad56e4b53e546ba031.gif" />


Of course, the "cool" factor played a significant role too. The visual allure of intricate green PCBs, complex wiring, and the seamless integration of tiny components was irresistible. Watching people confidently handle circuits with hundreds of connections felt awe-inspiring and intimidating—like staring up at an imposing mountain. That blend of aspiration and intimidation made me even more determined to become proficient.


Ultimately, this journey into electrical engineering has proven both daunting and rewarding. It has pushed my boundaries, expanded my horizons, and reinforced a crucial lesson: genuine skill diversity, the courage to dive into completely unfamiliar fields, and the adaptability to learn and grow are assets AI can never truly replicate or diminish.
